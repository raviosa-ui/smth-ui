Why every YouTube video sounds about the same volume — except yours

        
YouTube runs every upload through loudness normalization targeting -14 LUFS integrated. If your video is louder than that, YouTube quietly turns it down for the viewer. If it's quieter, nothing happens — your video simply plays softer than the one before it and the one after it, and viewers reach for the volume knob. The fix isn't guesswork or "turning it up in editing"; it's measuring your actual integrated loudness and true peak before you publish, the same way a mastering engineer checks a mix before release.

        
This tool decodes your video's audio track locally using the Web Audio API, resamples it to 48kHz, and runs a gated loudness measurement modeled on the ITU-R BS.1770 standard broadcasters use — the same family of math behind every professional loudness meter. It reports integrated loudness, true peak (checked with 4x oversampling to catch inter-sample peaks a normal meter misses), and a loudness-range figure, then walks the whole timeline in three-second windows to catch sections that swing loud or quiet relative to the rest of your own video, which YouTube's single-pass normalization can't fix for you.

        
Once you know what's wrong, the tool can fix the audio itself: a smoothed gain curve pulls the file toward -14 LUFS while specifically correcting the flagged loud and quiet sections, with a limiter guarding true peak. Preview it, download it as a WAV, or copy the pre-filled FFmpeg command — built from your file's actual measured numbers — to apply the same correction to your original video in seconds with -c:v copy, no video re-encode required.


        
Frequently Asked Questions

        

          

            What loudness level should my YouTube video be? ›
            
YouTube normalizes playback to -14 LUFS integrated loudness. If your video is louder than that, YouTube turns it down for viewers automatically. If it's quieter, YouTube does not turn it up — your video simply plays quieter than everything around it. Getting close to -14 LUFS yourself is the only way to control how loud your video actually sounds.

          

          

            Why do some of my videos sound quieter than others on YouTube? ›
            
YouTube only ever reduces loud videos to match its -14 LUFS target; it never boosts quiet ones. If one of your videos was mixed well below -14 LUFS, it will play noticeably quieter than your other uploads even though nothing else changed. Checking integrated loudness before publishing is the fix.

          

          

            What is true peak and why does -1 dBTP matter? ›
            
True peak measures the highest point your waveform actually reaches between samples, including peaks that only appear after the file is re-encoded. Keeping true peak at or below -1 dBTP leaves enough headroom that YouTube's transcoding doesn't introduce audible clipping or distortion that wasn't in your original file.

          

          

            Does this tool upload my video anywhere? ›
            
No. The audio track is decoded and measured entirely in your browser using the Web Audio API. Nothing is transmitted to any server, and the corrected audio file it generates is built and downloaded locally.

          

          

            Can this tool fix the audio automatically? ›
            
Yes for the audio track itself: click Compute Correction and the tool builds a smoothed gain curve that brings the file to -14 LUFS while evening out the loud and quiet sections, then protects true peak with a limiter. You can preview it, download it as a WAV, or copy the pre-filled FFmpeg command to apply the same correction directly to your original video file without re-encoding the picture.

          

          

            What file types and sizes does it support? ›
            
Any video or audio file your browser can decode: MP4, MOV, WebM, MP3, WAV, M4A and more. Because the audio is decoded and resampled in memory, very long files (well over an hour) may be slow — for those, export just the audio track and drop that in instead.
