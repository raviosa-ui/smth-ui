How to make a GIF from a video

  1. Drop in your video. It opens straight from your phone or computer, so there is no upload to wait for.
  2. Mark the clip. Drag the left handle to where the GIF should start and the right handle to where it should end. Press Preview clip to watch just that part on a loop.
  3. Choose the settings. The defaults — 480 pixels wide at 12 frames per second — suit most chats and posts. Pick a loop style, a shape, or type a caption if you want one.
  4. Press Make GIF. The tool reads each frame, picks the best colours for your clip and builds the file.
  5. Download. Check the preview and the file size. If it is too big for where you are sending it, press Make it smaller.

What makes a GIF file big, and how to shrink it

A GIF stores every frame as a separate picture, which is why a few seconds of video can turn into many megabytes. Three settings decide almost all of the size, and each one trades a little quality for a lot of space:

ChangeEffect on sizeWhat you give up
Halve the lengthAbout halfNothing, if the moment still fits
480 → 320 px wideRoughly halfFine detail and small text
15 → 10 fpsAbout a third lessSome smoothness in fast motion
256 → 64 coloursA little to a lotSmooth gradients

Start by trimming. Most GIFs that work are between two and six seconds, and cutting a second off the end saves more than any quality setting. Footage with a still background also compresses far better than a moving camera, because the tool only stores the parts of each frame that changed.

Forward, reverse and boomerang loops

A GIF repeats forever, so the jump from the last frame back to the first is the part people notice. Boomerang removes that jump by playing the clip forward and then backward, which makes a seamless loop from almost any short action. Reverse simply plays the clip backward, which suits pours, drops and anything that looks surprising undone. Changing the speed works the same way: 2× turns a slow process into a quick loop, and 0.5× gives a slow-motion replay without a larger file per second.

Why this converter does not upload your video

Most online GIF makers send your video to a server, convert it there and send the GIF back. That means waiting on your upload speed, accepting a file-size limit, and trusting someone else with the footage. This tool does the whole job in your browser. The video is decoded on your device, the colours are chosen from your own clip rather than a generic palette, and the finished GIF is saved directly to your downloads. Screen recordings with private details, client footage and unreleased clips never leave your machine.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I make a GIF from a video? ›
Drop your video into the tool, drag the two handles to mark where the GIF should start and end, and press Make GIF. When the preview appears, press Download GIF. You can change the size, frame rate, speed and loop style before or after the first try.
Is my video uploaded to a server? ›
No. The video is read by your browser straight from your device and the GIF is built on your own computer or phone. Nothing is transmitted, there is no account, and the GIF has no watermark.
How do I make the GIF file smaller? ›
The three things that matter most are length, width and frame rate. Shorten the clip, drop the width to 320 or 480 pixels, and use 10 or 12 frames per second. Fewer colours also helps. After a GIF is made, the Make it smaller button applies the next step down for you.
How long can the GIF be? ›
There is no fixed time limit, but GIFs grow quickly, so the tool caps a GIF at 500 frames. At 12 frames per second that is about 40 seconds; most good GIFs are 2 to 6 seconds long.
Which video formats can I convert to GIF? ›
Any video your browser can play: MP4 (H.264), WebM and most MOV files work everywhere. HEVC/H.265 clips from phones work in Safari and in recent Chrome and Edge on supported hardware. If a file will not open, re-export it as H.264 MP4.
What is a boomerang GIF? ›
A boomerang plays the clip forward and then backward, so the end joins the start with no visible jump. It suits short actions such as a wave, a pour or a head turn, and it makes a seamless loop out of footage that was never planned as one.
Why does my GIF look grainy or banded? ›
The GIF format can only show 256 colours at once, so smooth gradients such as skies and skin tones break into bands. Turn on Smooth gradients to blend them with a fine dot pattern. It looks better but makes the file larger.
